Where funny-card buyers search on Google, and what it's worth

Follow-up to our call on November 9. Google search demand, costs and competitors for humor greeting cards in the US, plus what larger card brands are running on Meta right now.

Short version

About 55,000 searches a month in the US are for funny greeting cards, and nobody was running a text ad on the two biggest terms when we checked. Clicks are cheap, around $0.90. The real Google upside is the December and June peaks, free and paid Shopping listings, and pushing searchers into bundles so their basket looks like your $35 average, not a single $5 card.

1. People search by occasion, not by attitude

Out of roughly 660,000 yearly searches for humor cards, only about 2% use words like sarcastic, snarky, rude, inappropriate or offensive. The rest are "funny" plus an occasion or a person: birthday, Christmas, Father's Day, sister, dad, 50th. Your brand voice is sassy; your customer's search is "funny birthday card for my sister." Ads, product titles and Shopping feeds should use the buyer's words.

2. Demand peaks twice a year, and the first peak starts now

Humor card searches nearly double in December and June. "Funny christmas cards" alone goes from about 8,000 searches in a normal month to about 60,000 in December. "Funny father's day cards" goes from about 3,600 to about 40,000 in June. November is already 30% above an average month.

3. The Google results page is open, but not empty

No text ads on the top terms Measured

On November 10, 2026, US searches for "funny birthday cards" and "funny christmas cards" showed no sponsored text ads. We checked twice, with two separate tools. Pretty Alright Goods and Lovepop both buy Google search ads on other terms (SpyFu, October 2026). Ink & Attitude runs none.

One brand owns the product grids Measured

Across five searches, Google showed 60 product listings. NobleWorks held 35 of them, almost 6 in 10. Hallmark, Comedy Card Company and PaperCards.com had 4 each, and Target had 3. These listings come from a free Google Merchant Center feed and cost nothing per click. Ink & Attitude did not appear anywhere on any of the five pages.

Prices match yours

Product-grid cards were priced almost entirely between $4.00 and $5.50, with NobleWorks at $5.48. Your $4–5 cards would compete on the joke and the photo, not on price. Measured

Weak spots in the results

Pinterest takes 9 of the website spots across these searches, and photo-card companies (Shutterfly, Zazzle, Canva) lead "funny christmas cards." Our inference: many of these pages don't sell a ready-made funny card, which leaves room for a store that does. Inference

This sizes a wider market of 2 million searches a year, as if you showed on every one. It is the size of the market, not a forecast of what you would earn.

Cross-check: the same WordStream report puts the average conversion rate for gift retailers at 4.01%, close to your 3.69%. Its average cost per click for that category is $4.14; card searches run about a fifth of that.

What this means: gross profit and ad spend come out roughly equal, at about $145,000–150,000 each. A bigger market makes the numbers bigger but doesn't change the ratio between them. On first orders alone, a humor search campaign would about break even. It becomes profitable only if Google customers reorder, if you pay less than the top-of-page estimate, or if their baskets match your $35 average.
